What's new  ·  October 2026

Macro close-ups, photos on your Mac, a steadier Mac mic, and a smoother preview.

New in TestFlight 1.2.3 for CamBridge on iPhone and CamBridge Studio on Mac. Here is what changed.

Macro, like the Camera app

Get close to something small and a yellow flower appears when the macro lens kicks in. Tap it to stay on the main camera. It stays off until you tap it again. This works from the Mac too.

Photos reach your Mac

Shots you take in PHOTO mode, at 12 MP or 48 MP, land in your Footage folder on the Mac as the original files.

A more reliable Mac mic

Switching mics now works every time. If a mic sends no sound, Studio warns you and keeps the iPhone's own audio, so you never end up with a silent video. Sync on long recordings is even more precise.

Smoother, and a simpler name

The Mac preview stays smooth when you start recording. The iPhone app is now simply called CamBridge.

Also in 1.2.2

One Microphone list in Studio

Pick an iPhone mic or a mic plugged into your Mac, all from one list. Choose a Mac mic and you get one finished video with that mic's sound, already in sync. There are no extra audio files to line up, and the leftovers go to the Trash. Your sound stays just as the mic recorded it, with no automatic volume changes.

Your footage stays private

Footage only goes to Macs you approve. The iPhone asks once for each Mac, and it checks that it is really your Mac.

PHOTO mode: 12 MP or 48 MP

Take photos at 12 MP (the default) or 48 MP. Change it on the iPhone or from your Mac.

Smooth lens switching

Switch between 0.5× · 1× · 3× and the picture glides from one lens to the next. The camera also starts more smoothly.

A new look

A new logo and new colours, with a short logo animation when the iPhone app opens.

Also in 1.2.1

Lock focus and exposure, then swipe to adjust

Press and hold the iPhone preview, or click and hold on the Mac, to lock focus and exposure like the native Camera app. Swipe up or down to brighten or darken. After a tap, focus goes back to auto when the scene changes.

Record your Mac's mic

Pick a USB or podcast mic in Studio and it records on the Mac while the iPhone films. In 1.2.2 this got simpler: you now get one finished video with that mic's sound.

Photos land on your Mac too

Photos, including ones you trigger from the Mac, now arrive in the Footage folder on your Mac, just like your videos.

A redesigned camera screen and Studio

The iPhone screen now looks like the native Camera app and stays readable over bright scenes. On the Mac, the controls are compact and float over the preview. Full screen is cleaner, with a grid and a level.

Fewer interruptions

A WhatsApp or phone call that takes the mic no longer causes a camera error. CamBridge keeps filming video only and recovers on its own after the call. The iPhone no longer drops into the StandBy clock while CamBridge is open. Full screen on the Mac exits cleanly with Esc, even after a disconnect. Lens order is now 0.5× · 1× · 3×.

Searching for a fix? You probably landed here from one of these.

If any of this sounds familiar, CamBridge was built for exactly this.

Continuity Camera forces my vertical video into landscape 16:9

Apple's Continuity Camera auto-rotates, stabilizes, and crops vertical footage into a horizontal frame. Great for webcam calls, useless for TikTok and Reels. CamBridge streams the true portrait frame straight from the iPhone. Nothing gets rotated or cropped.

QuickTime only shows my iPhone camera in landscape

Since Sonoma, using your iPhone as a camera source in QuickTime on macOS locks the preview to landscape, no matter how you're holding the phone. CamBridge is a native Mac monitor that follows your iPhone's actual orientation. Vertical stays vertical.

How do I take photos with my iPhone from my Mac?

See what the iPhone's camera sees, full-size, on your Mac. Tap the preview to pull focus, click a button to fire the shutter. True tethered shooting from your desk, no walking over to the phone.

iPhone Mirroring won't open the Camera app

iPhone Mirroring is built for everyday apps, not live camera control, so the Camera app simply isn't supported. CamBridge is built exactly for this gap: full remote camera control from your Mac, with a real live feed.

Camo, EpocCam, or Blackmagic Camera didn't solve it

Camo and EpocCam turn your iPhone into a webcam feed. EpocCam's remote can only snap pictures, not record video, and neither gives you real remote control. Blackmagic Camera's remote is iPhone-to-iPhone or iPad, not Mac. Screen Mirroring just takes over your whole display. CamBridge gives you real Mac-side remote control and real 4K files, recorded on the iPhone itself.

Remote control my iPhone camera from my MacBook to record myself

Put your iPhone on a tripod or a tall stand, walk back to your desk, and drive the whole shot from your MacBook: framing, focus, zoom, exposure, and the shutter, all without touching the phone again.

We built this because nothing else gave us both.

Camo Studio and Blackmagic Camera are both real products, both worth trying. CamBridge exists because neither one gave us portrait AND landscape monitoring with real, full remote control from the Mac, so we built it.

Straight answers, no marketing spin.

Do I need Wi‑Fi, or can I use a cable?

Either. CamBridge works over the same Wi‑Fi network or over a USB cable. Plug the iPhone into the Mac and Studio detects the cable automatically, and the link menu lets you choose Auto, Cable only or Wi‑Fi only. Recording is always the real 4K file on the iPhone, so the link only carries the live monitor.

Is this a finished app?

No — CamBridge is currently a TestFlight beta distributed through Apple's TestFlight program. That means Apple has reviewed the build, but it's still in active development, things can change, and you're trying it before general release. Join via the buttons on this page.

What quality actually gets recorded?

The real recording is full-quality 4K captured on the iPhone itself, and it's auto-transferred to your Mac when you stop. The live preview you see on your Mac while shooting is a separate, lighter monitoring feed — not the file that gets saved. Don't judge final quality by the live preview.

Does it work with my DJI wireless mic?

Yes — two ways. Connect the DJI Mic directly over Bluetooth (no receiver needed) and CamBridge uses it as the recording mic, or plug its receiver into the iPhone for the highest audio quality. One honest physics note: Bluetooth mic audio is voice-call quality on any app — the receiver path always sounds better. The mic's on-body record button reaches the phone only through the receiver (in Bluetooth-only mode DJI's button controls the mic's internal recorder, not your phone — that's a DJI hardware behavior, true in every app).

Does anything leave my network?

No. CamBridge has no accounts, no sign-up, and no cloud server in the middle — your iPhone and Mac talk directly over your local Wi‑Fi or a USB cable. See our privacy page for details.

What devices does this need?

An iPhone running iOS 17 or later, and a Mac running macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon or Intel).

Does it cost anything?

It's free during the beta. We don't have final pricing figured out yet — when that changes we'll be upfront about it, and people testing it now will be treated well. No specific promises beyond that.

Can I use more than one iPhone?

Yes. CamBridge Studio can connect to several iPhones at once and show them in a grid. Click a tile to open its program view, with per-phone controls and recording. Recordings land in Footage/ in a folder named after each phone. A second iPhone running CamBridge Camera can also act as the director's monitor and remote: it discovers cameras on the network or connects by IP, and the camera phone can require approval.

How do I report a bug or give feedback?

Email mohsin.sapra@gmail.com — real feedback from beta testers directly shapes what gets built next.

How can I use my Mac to remotely take photos with my iPhone?

Open CamBridge Camera on your iPhone and CamBridge Studio on your Mac — the live view shows up big on your Mac, you can tap anywhere in the preview to focus, and click the shutter button from your desk to trigger the photo on the iPhone. No accessories required, and a cable is optional.

Why does my vertical iPhone video turn horizontal on the Mac (Continuity Camera / QuickTime)?

Apple's Continuity Camera and QuickTime's iPhone camera source are built around a landscape webcam feed — since macOS Sonoma, they auto-rotate, stabilize, and crop vertical footage into 16:9 horizontal. CamBridge is a different kind of app: it's a dedicated Mac monitor and remote control for the iPhone camera, so it streams and shows the true portrait frame with no rotation or cropping.

Is CamBridge a webcam app like Camo or EpocCam?

No. Camo and EpocCam turn your iPhone into a webcam feed for calls and streams. CamBridge is a remote camera and monitor system — the recording you get is the real 4K camera file saved on the iPhone itself, then auto-transferred to your Mac, not a lower-quality webcam stream.
