How to get full-quality 4K iPhone videos onto your Mac automatically

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

4K video files are large. Moving them from an iPhone to a Mac by hand means AirDrop, a cable and Photos, or iCloud. It is easy to forget a clip, or to end up with a compressed copy.

If you shoot a lot, you want the original file in a known folder as soon as you stop recording.

Quick answer

Use CamBridge. Choose a Footage folder in Studio. When you stop recording on the iPhone, the full-quality 4K HEVC file copies to that folder, in a subfolder for that phone. Photos copy too. You can keep a copy on the iPhone or not.

Why the file matters more than the preview

Many iPhone-to-Mac tools send you a live picture. That is fine for a call and poor for a deliverable. When the camera saves the file on the phone and then copies the original across, you get the same bits the sensor and encoder produced. Nothing is recompressed on the way.

It also removes a chore. You finish a take, and by the time you sit down to edit, the clip is already in your project folder. With many phones, the per-phone subfolders keep them apart.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need CamBridge Camera (iOS 17+) and CamBridge Studio (macOS 14+) running together, with the phone on the same Wi‑Fi or connected by cable.

  1. Pick your Footage folder

    In Studio, choose the folder where clips should land. A fast external drive works well for 4K.

  2. Connect the iPhone

    Use Wi‑Fi or a USB cable. Studio detects the cable. The link menu offers Auto, Cable only or Wi‑Fi only.

  3. Choose quality settings

    Pick 4K and 24, 30 or 60 fps. SDR is the default. Optional 10-bit HLG HDR is available. The untouched 16:9 master is always kept.

  4. Decide on the iPhone copy

    Turn on keep a copy on iPhone if you want a second copy on the phone. Leave it off to save space.

  5. Record

    Start and stop from Studio or the phone. The recording is made on the iPhone itself.

  6. Let it copy

    When you stop, the file transfers to your Footage folder. A cable is faster than Wi‑Fi for big clips. Photos are transferred too.

  7. Open the file

    The file is the original camera file, not a stream capture. Drop it straight into your editor.

Without CamBridge

  • AirDrop. Quick for single files. Select the clip in Photos, share, choose your Mac. Apple's current options for choosing quality when sharing may vary, so check Photos on your device.
  • Photos with iCloud. Photos can sync your library. Originals can be kept on the device or optimized, and storage depends on your plan. Details are in Apple's iCloud Photos documentation.
  • Image Capture or the Photos app over USB. Plug the phone in and import. Reliable, and manual.
  • Files app and a network drive. Works if you have one set up.

All of these are free and built in. They suit occasional use. They need you to remember to do them.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Use a cable for long clips. It is faster than Wi‑Fi.
  • Check free space on the Mac before a shoot. 4K uses a lot.
  • Back up the Footage folder before you edit.
  • If the transfer is interrupted, check that the phone and Mac apps are still open.

Limits to know about

The transfer needs both apps and a connection. It copies what CamBridge recorded. It does not import your older camera roll. There is no cloud backup. 4:3 photos at 12 and 48 MP are coming next but are not released yet.

FAQ

Is the transferred file really 4K?

Yes. The recording is made on the iPhone and the full-quality file is copied.

Does the live preview affect quality?

No. The preview is a separate lighter feed.

Can I transfer over USB?

Yes. Studio detects the cable automatically.

Does it copy photos too?

Yes.

Can I keep the video on the iPhone as well?

Yes, with the keep a copy on iPhone option.

Does it import old videos from my camera roll?

No. It handles what you record with CamBridge Camera.
