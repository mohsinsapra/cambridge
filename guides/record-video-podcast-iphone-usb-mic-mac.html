How to record a video podcast with an iPhone camera and a USB mic on your Mac

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

A video podcast needs two things that live in different places. The best camera you own is on your iPhone. The best microphone you own is probably a USB or XLR podcast mic plugged into your Mac.

Normally you would record both separately and sync them by hand. CamBridge does the sync for you.

Quick answer

In Studio, open the Microphone list and pick your USB mic. Record as usual. When the transfer ends you get one finished video with your Mac mic's sound, already in sync. The leftover files go to the Trash.

Why record the mic on the Mac

The iPhone's rear camera beats most webcams by a wide margin. A dedicated USB or podcast mic beats the phone's built-in mic by a similar margin. Using both gives you a video podcast that looks and sounds professional, without buying a camera.

The catch is that the two recordings start at slightly different moments. Lining them up by hand takes time. CamBridge does it for you, so the finished video stays in sync from start to end.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need CamBridge 1.2.2 or later on both devices, a USB or podcast microphone connected to the Mac, and an iPhone on a tripod.

  1. Plug the mic into the Mac

    Connect your USB or podcast microphone. Set it up as you normally do, with the gain at a safe level.

  2. Pick the mic in the Microphone list

    Open the Microphone list in Studio. It shows the iPhone mics and the mics on your Mac. Choose your Mac mic. A level meter shows the sound, so you can check that it moves when you speak.

  3. Leave everything else as it is

    You do not need to change any iPhone audio setting. CamBridge does the syncing for you.

  4. Frame the shot

    Mount the iPhone, connect over Wi‑Fi or USB, and check the preview in Studio. Click to focus and set exposure. For a vertical clip, set portrait capture orientation.

  5. Record

    Press Space in Studio. The iPhone records 4K video, and the Mac records your mic at the same time.

  6. Stop and wait for the transfer

    The video copies to your Footage folder. Then CamBridge builds your finished video.

  7. Use your finished video

    Open the new video in your Footage folder. It has your Mac mic's sound, already in sync. The extra files used to build it go to the Trash, so there is nothing to line up by hand. Your sound stays just as the mic recorded it, with no automatic volume changes.

Without CamBridge

  • Clap sync. Record the camera and mic separately and line them up with a clap or waveform in your editor. Free, and it works, but it is manual and drifts on long recordings.
  • Wire the mic to the iPhone. A USB or Lightning adapter lets the phone record the mic directly. You lose the Mac-side recording flow. See the DJI Mic 2 guide for wireless options.
  • Continuity Camera with a podcast app. The Mac app picks the camera and the mic. You record a webcam-quality stream rather than the iPhone's 4K file.
  • Dedicated recorders. Record audio on a separate device and sync in the edit. Common in studios.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Do a 10 second test and play back the synced clip before a long session.
  • Do not unplug the USB mic mid-recording.
  • For guests in the same room, record each person on their own phone. See multi-iPhone recording.

Limits to know about

The Mac mic feature is in the 1.2.2 beta. The finished video appears after the transfer, not live. It records one Mac mic at a time. It does not record a live mix of several mics.

FAQ

Which mics work?

USB and podcast microphones the Mac recognizes, listed in Studio's Microphone list.

Do I get extra files to line up?

No. You get one finished video with your Mac mic's sound, already in sync. The leftover files go to the Trash.

How is the audio synced?

Automatically. CamBridge lines up the Mac sound with the video for you.

Does CamBridge change my volume?

No. The sound is kept exactly as your mic recorded it, with no automatic volume changes.

Do I need the cable?

No. Wi‑Fi or USB both work for the live link.

Can I use this for a live stream?

No. CamBridge records files. It is not a virtual camera.
