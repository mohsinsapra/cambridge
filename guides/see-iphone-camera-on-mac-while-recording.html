Recording yourself guide

How to see your iPhone camera on your Mac while you record yourself

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

You want to film yourself with your iPhone's good rear camera. The problem is that the screen faces away from you. You cannot check framing, focus or lighting without reaching for the phone or filming blind and fixing it in the next take.

The fix is a live monitor. The phone records, and a bigger screen next to you shows what it sees. This guide shows how to do that with your Mac, using CamBridge, and what your options are if you would rather not install anything.

CamBridge is a free TestFlight beta, so a few things are still changing. Where I describe a limit, I say so.

Quick answer

Install CamBridge Camera on the iPhone and CamBridge Studio on the Mac. Put both on the same Wi‑Fi, or connect a USB cable. The Studio window shows a live HEVC preview from the iPhone. Press Space on the Mac to record. The full 4K file is saved on the iPhone and copied to your Mac when you stop.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need an iPhone on iOS 17 or later, a Mac on macOS 14 or later, and a tripod or phone mount. Both apps are in the free TestFlight beta.

  1. Join the two TestFlight betas

    Open the iPhone link on your phone and the Mac link on your Mac. Install CamBridge Camera and CamBridge Studio. Both links are at the bottom of this page.

  2. Mount the iPhone with the rear camera facing you

    Use a tripod, a desk mount or a stack of books. The rear camera is the one with the best sensor and lenses, which is the whole point. Leave the phone unlocked and the Camera app open.

  3. Connect over Wi‑Fi or a cable

    On the same Wi‑Fi network, Studio finds the phone by itself. For a steadier picture, plug the iPhone into the Mac with USB. Studio detects the cable automatically. The link menu has three choices: Auto, Cable only and Wi‑Fi only.

  4. Check your framing on the Mac

    The live preview appears in the Studio window. It is a lighter monitoring feed, not the final file, so do not judge sharpness from it. Use it for framing, exposure and background.

  5. Adjust from your chair

    Click in the preview to focus. Drag for exposure, or use the exposure EV control. Pick the 0.5×, 1× or 3× lens, turn on the grid or level, and switch the torch on if you need light. You never touch the phone.

  6. Record and stop

    Press Space in Studio to start and stop. Press Esc twice to discard a take. You can also press a volume button on the iPhone itself. The phone records real 4K HEVC on-device.

  7. Find the clip on your Mac

    When you stop, the full-quality file transfers to your chosen Footage folder, in a subfolder for that phone. You can also keep a copy on the iPhone if you want one.

Without CamBridge

You have other ways to get a preview. They each trade something away.

  • Continuity Camera. Apple lets a Mac app use your iPhone as a webcam. It needs an iPhone XR or later, iOS 16 or later and macOS Ventura 13 or later, with both devices signed in to the same Apple Account (Apple support). It is made for calls and for apps that record from a camera. You get a webcam stream, not the iPhone's 4K camera file. See Continuity Camera vs CamBridge.
  • QuickTime Player on the Mac. A USB-connected iPhone can be a movie recording source. It is a simple option, but you get no remote control of focus or exposure.
  • Mirror via a screen-sharing tool. Works for a rough check, but adds delay and you still film with the front camera or hand-hold the phone.
  • The iPhone front camera. You see yourself, but the front camera is weaker than the rear one.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Use the cable if the preview stutters. A busy Wi‑Fi network is the usual cause.
  • Put the monitor near the lens. If you look at the Mac window, your eyes will point away from the camera.
  • Lock exposure with a click-and-hold AE/AF lock before you start talking, so the picture does not pump.
  • If a phone call or WhatsApp call takes the microphone, CamBridge keeps filming video-only and recovers afterwards.

Limits to know about

CamBridge is not a webcam. It does not appear as a camera in Zoom, FaceTime or OBS. For calls and streams, use Continuity Camera or Camo. It runs on iPhone and Mac only, with no Android or Windows version. It is a beta, so expect rough edges.

FAQ

Can I watch my iPhone camera on my Mac without recording?

Yes. The live monitor works on its own. You can frame, focus and zoom without pressing record.

Is there lag in the preview?

There is some, as with any wireless monitor. A USB cable is the lowest-delay option. Recording is not affected, because the file is captured on the iPhone.

Is the Mac preview the quality I will record?

No. The preview is a lighter HEVC feed (with a JPEG fallback). The recording is real 4K on the phone.

Do I need an account?

No. CamBridge has no accounts and no cloud.

Does it work with the front camera?

The guide above uses the rear camera because it is the better one. Check the beta for the current camera options.

Can I use it for Zoom?

No. CamBridge is not a virtual camera. Use Continuity Camera or Camo for calls.
