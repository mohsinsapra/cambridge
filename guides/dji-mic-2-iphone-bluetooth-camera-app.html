How to use a DJI Mic 2 with an iPhone camera app (Bluetooth or receiver)

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

The DJI Mic 2 is a popular wireless mic for phone video. You can connect it to an iPhone in two ways, and they do not sound the same.

This guide explains both, shows how to pick the mic inside CamBridge Camera, and tells you when to use each.

Quick answer

Direct Bluetooth works with no extra gear but uses the phone's hands-free profile (HFP), which is voice-call quality. For full quality, plug the Mic 2 receiver into the iPhone and select it as the input. In CamBridge, you choose the iPhone mic from Studio on your Mac, and a live level meter shows the signal.

Bluetooth versus receiver in plain terms

When an iPhone talks to a Bluetooth mic, iOS may use the hands-free profile, the same one used for phone calls. It is built for speech over a narrow link, so the audio sounds thinner than a wired mic. A receiver in the charging port behaves like a wired microphone, so the audio keeps its full range.

For quick social clips, the difference may not matter. For interviews, music or anything you will mix, plug in the receiver.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need a DJI Mic 2 transmitter, the matching receiver with the right connector for your iPhone, and CamBridge Camera on the phone. Studio on the Mac is optional but handy for the level meter and mic selection.

  1. Decide: Bluetooth or receiver

    Bluetooth is the fastest setup. The receiver gives the best sound. If this is for a talking-head clip and you are in a hurry, Bluetooth is fine. For a podcast or anything you will mix, use the receiver.

  2. Option A: pair over Bluetooth

    Put the transmitter in pairing mode and pair it in iOS Bluetooth settings, following DJI's instructions. The Mic 2 connects to the iPhone directly with no receiver. iOS treats it as a hands-free device, so the audio is voice-call quality.

  3. Option B: use the receiver

    Plug the receiver into the iPhone using the connector that matches your phone's port (USB-C or Lightning). Switch it on and make sure it is linked to the transmitter. This route records at full quality.

  4. Open CamBridge Camera

    Start the camera app on the iPhone. Connect Studio over Wi‑Fi or USB if you want to control it from the Mac.

  5. Select the mic

    Choose the DJI Mic 2 in the iPhone mic selection. You can do this from Studio on the Mac. Speak, and watch the live level meter. Aim for strong peaks that do not hit the top.

  6. Record a short test

    Record 10 seconds, stop, and listen to the clip when it arrives in your Footage folder. Then shoot the real take.

  7. Keep an eye on calls

    If a call (for example WhatsApp) takes the microphone, CamBridge keeps filming video-only and recovers when the call ends. Check your audio afterwards.

Without CamBridge

  • The iPhone's own mic. Fine at arm's length. Poor in a room or in wind.
  • The stock Camera app. It can use external mics too. You get the same trade-off between Bluetooth and the receiver.
  • A USB mic into the Mac. Better for sit-down shows. CamBridge records it on the Mac and syncs it. See the video podcast guide.
  • Record audio on the transmitter. The Mic 2 transmitters can record internally (check DJI's specs for your setup). Sync in the edit as a backup.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Clip the transmitter 15 to 20 cm from your mouth, away from clothes that rub.
  • If Bluetooth audio sounds thin, that is the hands-free profile. The receiver is the fix.
  • Test indoors and out. Wireless range and interference vary.
  • Charge the transmitters before long sessions.

Limits to know about

CamBridge selects the iPhone's audio input. It does not configure the DJI hardware, so follow DJI's own guide for pairing and firmware. Bluetooth hands-free audio is call quality on iPhone, and a receiver is the way around that.

FAQ

Can the DJI Mic 2 connect to an iPhone without the receiver?

Yes, over Bluetooth. Expect call-quality audio because of the hands-free profile.

Which gives better sound?

The receiver plugged into the iPhone.

Does CamBridge work with the Mic 2?

Yes. It supports the DJI Mic 2 directly over Bluetooth or via its receiver, plus USB and wired mics.

Can I see the audio level on my Mac?

Yes. Studio shows a live level meter.

What if a phone call comes in?

CamBridge keeps filming video-only and recovers.

Can I use a DJI Mic 2 on the Mac side instead?

Yes. If the Mac sees it as a USB audio device, pick it in Studio's Microphone list. Check DJI's documentation for the Mac connection mode.
