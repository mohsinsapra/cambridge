Recording yourself guide

How to film vertical TikTok, Reels and Shorts with your iPhone and monitor on your Mac

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

Vertical video is the default for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. The trouble starts when you want a bigger monitor than the phone screen. Webcam-style tools on the Mac are built around a wide frame, so a tall shot often ends up cropped, rotated or letterboxed.

This guide shows how to shoot real portrait video with the iPhone's main camera and watch it on your Mac at the same time.

The short version: record on the phone, not through a webcam pipe. Then the file is whatever the camera saved.

Quick answer

Open CamBridge Camera, set the capture orientation to portrait, and mount the phone upright. Connect Studio on the Mac. The preview is vertical. Record with Space. The file keeps the full 4K quality and arrives in your Footage folder. The untouched 16:9 master is always kept, even if you choose a different aspect ratio.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need CamBridge Camera on an iPhone (iOS 17+) and CamBridge Studio on a Mac (macOS 14+). A tripod with a vertical mount helps.

  1. Mount the iPhone upright

    Use a tripod head that holds the phone in portrait. Keep the rear camera at eye level, a little above if you can.

  2. Choose portrait capture orientation

    In CamBridge Camera, set the capture orientation explicitly to portrait. You pick it yourself rather than letting the phone guess from how it is tilted. Landscape is a choice too, so the same setup works for YouTube.

  3. Open Studio and connect

    Studio finds the phone over Wi‑Fi, or over a USB cable if you plug one in. The link menu offers Auto, Cable only and Wi‑Fi only.

  4. Pick resolution, frame rate and aspect

    Choose 4K at 24, 30 or 60 fps. Aspect options are 16:9, 4:3 and 1:1. SDR is the default. There is an optional 10-bit HLG HDR mode. Whatever you pick, the untouched 16:9 master is kept as well.

  5. Frame from the Mac

    The monitor shows a tall frame. Turn on the grid and level to check headroom and tilt. Click to focus on your face. Drag for exposure.

  6. Record

    Press Space to start and stop. Esc twice discards the take. The volume buttons on the phone also start and stop recording.

  7. Edit and post

    The clip lands in your Footage folder on the Mac. Open it in your editor of choice. Export at your target platform's size.

Without CamBridge

  • Continuity Camera. Apple's page says to mount the iPhone in landscape so apps can choose it automatically, or in portrait (Apple support). It is a webcam feed, and most Mac apps present it as a wide video frame, so it is a poor fit for a clean vertical master. Check how your particular app handles portrait before you rely on it.
  • QuickTime Player. You can record from a connected iPhone, but there is no remote control, and what you see depends on the app.
  • Phone on a tripod, no monitor. Fine for casual shots. Hard to check focus on yourself.
  • Blackmagic Camera. A strong iPhone camera app with manual controls. It is a phone-side app, with no Mac studio for monitoring. See the comparison.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Leave space around the subject. You can crop to 9:16 in the edit, but you cannot add pixels that were never shot.
  • Shoot at 30 fps for talking-head work. Use 60 fps if you plan to slow motion down.
  • If you use a Mac microphone for better sound, see the podcast guide.
  • Pick the orientation before you press record. Changing it mid-take is not something to rely on.

Limits to know about

CamBridge does not publish to TikTok, Instagram or YouTube. It is a camera and a transfer tool. It is also not a webcam or virtual camera, so it will not feed a live vertical stream into OBS. It is an iPhone and Mac tool only, and it is a beta.

FAQ

Can I really film vertical video and watch it on my Mac?

Yes. Set portrait capture orientation in CamBridge Camera and the Studio monitor shows the vertical frame.

Why does Continuity Camera not suit vertical video?

It is designed as a webcam for calls and camera-using apps. Apps usually show it as a wide frame. Apple's page lists portrait as a mounting option, but how a given app uses it is up to the app.

What resolution are Reels and Shorts?

Platforms re-encode what you upload. Shooting in 4K gives you room to crop and reframe in the edit.

What does the 1:1 option do?

It records a square frame. The 16:9 master is still kept.

Do I have to use Wi‑Fi?

No. Plug in USB and Studio detects the cable.
