Recording yourself guide

How to control your iPhone camera remotely from your Mac (focus, exposure, zoom, record)

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

If you film yourself, you spend half the time walking back and forth to the phone. Tap to focus, step back, check, walk up again. Remote control removes that loop.

This guide covers what you can control from a Mac, how to do it with CamBridge, and what the built-in options give you instead.

Quick answer

With CamBridge Studio open and the iPhone connected, click in the live view to focus. Click and hold to lock AE/AF, then drag to set exposure. Use the zoom, lens (0.5×, 1×, 3×), torch, resolution, frame rate and stabilization controls in Studio. Press Space to record.

Why remote control matters

Self-shooting has a loop: set up, press record, walk back, notice the focus landed on the wall, walk up, fix it, repeat. Each pass costs a minute and some patience. When focus, exposure and zoom are on the Mac, the loop shrinks to a click. The bigger monitor also shows problems the phone screen hides, like a stray cable or a shadow across your face.

The phone stays the camera. Your commands go to it, the sensor does the work, and the file is saved on the phone. The Mac is the control surface and the monitor.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need the CamBridge Camera app on an iPhone (iOS 17+) and CamBridge Studio on a Mac (macOS 14+), connected by Wi‑Fi or USB.

  1. Connect the phone to Studio

    Open CamBridge Camera on the iPhone and Studio on the Mac. The phone shows up in Studio. Plug in a cable if you want a steadier link. Choose Auto, Cable only or Wi‑Fi only in the link menu.

  2. Click to focus

    Click the part of the picture that should be sharp, usually your eyes or the product. The phone refocuses there.

  3. Lock focus and exposure

    Click and hold to set the AE/AF lock. Then drag to move exposure up or down. You can do the same on the phone itself by pressing and holding, then swiping.

  4. Set exposure and zoom

    Use the exposure EV control for fine changes. Zoom with the zoom control, or jump between the 0.5×, 1× and 3× lenses.

  5. Set the camera options

    Change resolution, frame rate (24, 30 or 60), and stabilization. Add the grid and level for framing help. Turn the torch on for fill light.

  6. Choose the audio input

    Pick which iPhone microphone to use. A DJI Mic 2 or USB mic can be selected here. The level meter shows if the sound is too hot.

  7. Record, stop, discard

    Press Space to start and stop. Press Esc twice to discard. Photos can be taken from the Mac as well. Files then copy to your Footage folder.

Without CamBridge

  • The iPhone Camera app and a timer. Free and simple. You still cannot see the shot from a distance.
  • Apple Watch as a remote viewfinder. Apple's Camera Remote on Apple Watch gives a small preview and a shutter. It is not a Mac workflow, and the screen is tiny. Check Apple's documentation for current features.
  • Continuity Camera. It makes the iPhone a Mac camera input, but focus and exposure are managed by the Mac app and macOS, not by a full camera control panel. See the comparison guide.
  • Blackmagic Camera app. Excellent manual controls on the phone. Remote control is oriented around the phone, not a Mac studio. See the comparison.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Set exposure before you lock it, then lock. Auto exposure that drifts mid-take is the most common problem.
  • Stabilization is useful for handheld, but on a tripod you can leave it as is.
  • Use a cable when you are changing controls constantly. It is the most responsive link.
  • Keep the iPhone awake and unlocked in the camera screen.

Limits to know about

Remote control works while CamBridge Camera is in front on the iPhone. It is a beta, and the control set will change. It does not control other camera apps. It is iPhone and Mac only.

FAQ

Can I control focus on my iPhone from my Mac?

Yes. In CamBridge Studio, click in the live view to focus. Click and hold to lock AE/AF.

Can I start and stop recording from the Mac?

Yes. Space starts and stops. Esc twice discards the take.

Can I switch lenses remotely?

Yes. Use the 0.5×, 1× and 3× controls. What you see depends on which lenses your iPhone has.

Does the remote control add delay to what is recorded?

The recording happens on the phone, so the file is not affected by the live link.

Can I take photos from the Mac?

Yes. Photos are taken on the iPhone and transferred to the Mac too.

Does it work with other camera apps?

No. It controls CamBridge Camera only.
